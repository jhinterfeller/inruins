Tom Yum

Khukhan, Sisaket • 01

Went for a walk and decided to go to a restaurant that I hadn't been for a while. I started cooking myself and found it very easy to create a large menu with the products available at the markets. They have it all here in Khukhan if you just go looking. Fruits vegetables seafood and meats, all have their own markets.

When I arrived, she informed me that there would be a thirty-minute wait because someone called in an order. I went back about thirty minutes later and her seating was full of soldiers and one EMT. There is a festival in the making and many new people from around the province are setting up. They made room for me in the sweltering heat. I had ordered a tom yum soup and it was the wrong day for that. I sweat it out reading on my phone a book that I borrowed from the Olympia library. Chess Story by Stefan Zweig, starts off with people boarding a steamer heading to south America.

I can hear them joking about a farang with the chef and it is all in good humor, not in a condescending way. Then eight students walked past the front of the shop and saw me. They waved and held up kind hand signals that gave me street cred with the diners. I continued to read wondering if this boy in the story had dyslexia or something that was not recognized at the time of the writing.

The heat had me making several trips to the ice chest. It was a small cooler like the one you might take a lunch in if you are a construction worker or similar tradesman. My nose tingled, instant warning, don’t breathe in. Short measured whiffs of air, one deep breath and it’s over. Surrounded by fit mid-career military men, I didn’t want to lose composure and choke and die right there, the EMT would surely save me. The sweat was pouring out faster than I could process the water. Tiny breath, sting, don’t breathe. She was cooking some blazing pad krapow. The Thai chili is about 40 times spicier than the jalapeno. That capsaicin was floating around the restaurant.

I couldn’t help but think about how these people that surround me grew up to this. In their own homes as babies their mothers would launch these little capsaicin bombs of love probably every day. I think they breathe in this bear spray and it is to them what fresh baked bread on a holiday at grandma’s house is to me.

The soup came and it was mild, she knows I am weaker and she reduces the spice to pale like my skin. She came back many times to see if everything was ok. Then a lottery seller was walking by with her folding tray open to entice the passerby with a lucky number. The chef always thinks it is lucky when a farang visits her shop, so she buys a ticket. I hope she wins; she deserves a break.
